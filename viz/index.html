What the numbers under each grid mean

capVendi
Caption–Vendi. A VLM describes each image on this prompt's own human axis, and the Vendi score over those descriptions says how many distinct answers there effectively were (1–4).
gridScore
A VLM looks at the 2×2 grid and rates 1–5 how varied it is on that same axis, scored by expected value rather than the top choice.
TPIPS
Text-conditioned perceptual distance on that axis (0–1, higher = more varied). The axis phrase enters the image encoder itself, so unlike the two above it never passes through generated language.
TPIPS/o
The same distance divided by the unconditioned distance between the same four images — the share of a method's diversity that landed on the requested axis, rather than how much there was. This is the one worth reading: a method can raise TPIPS while lowering TPIPS/o, which means it bought variety somewhere nobody asked for. It sits below 1 for every method (conditioning on one axis always leaves fewer ways to differ), so compare against vanilla, not against 1.
DINO
DINOv2–Vendi — axis-blind diversity, included as the contrast. Where it disagrees with the axis-aware three is the interesting part.

Under each grid the small figure is the change against vanilla on the same prompt and model, which is the only comparison that means anything at single-prompt granularity. Pooled over all four scored models, prompt_expand is the one method that raises the axis share (about +10% over vanilla); cads and group_inference raise raw diversity but lower the share.
